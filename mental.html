Mental Model Lab

Train yourself to diverge first — then converge.

This lab trains a practical two-model habit: use one model to open possibilities, and a second model to challenge your assumptions before you close the explanation. The goal is not to become endlessly uncertain. The goal is to reduce premature judgment.

🧠 Two-model reasoning

Model A + Model B

Model A — Divergent

Ask: “What else could explain this?” Generate at least three plausible hypotheses. Search for hidden variables.

Model B — Inclusive / Husnuzan-Tepa Selira Lens

Ask: “What context might I be missing?” Separate observation from interpretation. Consider the other person's constraints before assigning motive.

Your reasoning loop

Practice the switch

1
Observe
What actually happened?

2
Diverge
What else could explain it?

3
Investigate
What information would distinguish the hypotheses?
4
Converge
Which explanation is best supported?

5
Close
Adopt a working model.

6
Reopen
What new evidence would change your mind?

Training principle: divergence without convergence can become analysis paralysis; convergence without divergence can become premature judgment.

Final prompt

Before you judge someone, ask two questions.

MODEL A — DIVERGE
“What else could explain this?”
MODEL B — CONTEXT
“What might I not know about their context?”

Then investigate. Then converge. You are not training yourself to trust everyone. You are training yourself to avoid treating your first interpretation as established fact.
