Divergence × Convergence

Think wider.

Invergent is an experimental space for understanding and training the two complementary movements of thinking: opening the possibility space, then narrowing it into a meaningful direction. The results are heuristic reflections, not definitive measures of a person's thinking ability. This is not a test of “who you are”; it is an estimate of which thinking mode may feel more natural in this set of tasks — opening possibilities (divergent) or narrowing them into decisions (convergent).

About the timer: It is a maximum time, not a target. When you finish an essay response, continue immediately — you do not need to wait for the timer to reach zero.

Before you begin

What is this actually measuring?

Invergent does not tell you what “type of person” you are. It is a heuristic reflection on how your responses behave across different thinking tasks. In other words, it looks for which mode may feel more natural or comfortable in this task set.

↗

Divergent

How naturally do you open the possibility space — generating alternatives, associations, perspectives, and unusual ideas?

↘

Convergent

How naturally do you narrow the possibility space — comparing options, applying constraints, and choosing a direction?

⇄

Switching

How easily do you move from “let’s explore” to “now let’s decide” when the task asks you to change gears?

One important distinction: finishing a task quickly does not automatically make a response convergent. Convergence is about narrowing, comparing, prioritizing, and choosing — not simply being fast. The timer is a maximum, not a target, so when you are done, you can continue immediately.
